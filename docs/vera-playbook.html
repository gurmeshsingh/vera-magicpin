Internal reference · working document

Building Vera Better

Everything known about the magicpin AI Challenge: the contract, the dataset, the scoring function, and the decisions we have made — and have not yet made — about how to win it.

01What we are building

A live, stateful HTTP service that plays magicpin's merchant-AI assistant, judged in real time by an LLM harness that pretends to be an Indian merchant on WhatsApp.

magicpin is a local-commerce platform with roughly 100,000 merchant partners across 50+ Indian cities. Vera is their existing WhatsApp assistant: it talks to 6,000–10,000 merchants a day, improves their Google Business Profiles, runs campaigns, and answers customer questions on their behalf. The challenge is to rebuild Vera's message brain and beat the production system.

The thing most entrants will get wrong

The public brief describes submitting bot.py plus a 30-line submission.jsonl. That is the older, offline framing. The submission form asks for a URL, and the testing brief defines a live service that is polled, pushed to, and conversed with for 60 simulated minutes. Build a server, not a file.

The core abstraction stays the same either way:

compose(category, merchant, trigger, customer?) → {
    body,            the WhatsApp message text
    cta,             binary YES/STOP · open_ended · none
    send_as,         "vera" | "merchant_on_behalf"
    suppression_key, dedupe identity for this topic+period
    rationale        why this message — and this IS scored
}

02The API contract

Five endpoints, JSON in and out, UTF-8, reachable on the public internet. The bot is stateful and must not restart mid-test.

Hard limits

LimitValueDesign consequence
Per-call timeout30 sCannot run 20 sequential LLM calls in one tick

If a tick will exceed 30 s, return {"actions": []} immediately. The FAQ is explicit that you cannot background-process and answer late — late responses are dropped, and you cannot catch up on a later tick. Design to the budget.

03Judge lifecycle

Two information flows run in opposite directions: the judge pushes context down, and the bot pushes messages up into a conversation the judge role-plays.

The five phases

04How points are won

The five rubric dimensions are what everyone reads. They are not where the contest is decided.

DimensionThe judge is asking
SpecificityDoes this anchor on a concrete, checkable fact — a number, date, headline, peer stat — rather than "grow your sales"?
Category fitDoes a dentist message sound clinical-peer, and a salon message visual? Correct vocabulary, no taboo words?
Merchant fitIs this this merchant — their numbers, their active offers, their history, their language?
Trigger relevance
aka decision_quality		Is it obvious why now? Does the message name the event that prompted it?
Engagement compulsionWould a real, busy merchant actually reply? One strong reason, one low-effort action.
Naming collision, resolved

The rubric prose says trigger relevance; the judge's JSON schema field is decision_quality. The parser accepts either key for the same slot. They are one dimension, and it means: did you use the trigger payload and justify why-now.

The full score surface

SourceRangeWho will actually contest it
Per-message rubric, 5 dims × 0–1050 / msgEveryone. This is the crowded field.
Phase 3 adaptation bonus, +5 per dimension+25Few Requires version diffing that most will not build
Phase 4 replay, top 10 only+30Ten teams Gated behind Phase 2
Operational penalties−20Everyone loses some Timeouts, malformed JSON, repeats

Roughly a third of the achievable score is not copywriting. It is reacting to injected context, holding a conversation, and never timing out. That asymmetry is the strategy.

Cheap points almost everyone forfeits

  • The rationale field is scored. The FAQ says so directly — the judge checks whether the rationale matches the output, and a good one makes it "interpret edge cases generously." Most entrants will send a stub string.
  • Restraint is rewarded. {"actions": []} is explicitly legitimate. An unsent message scores nothing; a weak one costs rubric points and courts the repetition penalty.
  • Anti-repetition is −2 per verbatim resend inside a conversation.
  • send_as correctness — vera when talking to the merchant, merchant_on_behalf when writing to their customer. Phase 3 injects five customer contexts precisely to test this.
  • Language match. Honour identity.languages. Sending pure English to a hi merchant is a named anti-pattern.

05The 4-context model

Every message Vera sends is composed from four layers of context, arriving at different speeds. Understanding which layer is reliable and which is sparse is the single most important thing in this document.

07What the data really looks like

The brief's showcase example is a hand-authored seed record. It is not representative of the surface you will be scored on.

The official generator is deterministic and stdlib-only. We ran it. Here is the real shape of the 50 merchants and 100 triggers it produces:

86%merchants with no offers
84%merchants with no conversation history
80%merchants with no derived signals
75%triggers with a placeholder payload
70%triggers with no customer attached
The trap this sets

Read the brief and your instinct is: pull specificity out of the trigger payload. Dr. Meera's example trigger carries a JIDA citation, a trial size, and a 38% figure right there in payload.top_item.

For three quarters of triggers there is nothing in the payload to pull. A bot architected around trigger-payload specificity produces hollow, generic copy on most inputs — and loses the highest-signal dimension on the majority of its sends.

Other sharp edges

  • 8 of 26 trigger kinds never appear in the canonical test pairs — including renewal_due, research_digest and review_theme_emerged. Since the real judge uses fresh scenarios, tuning on test_pairs.json leaves gaps exactly where the unseen set may probe.
  • 10 kinds exist as a single seed instance each. One is the lone urgency-5 case: a pharmacy supply/recall alert. Statistically negligible, operationally the most consequential message in the dataset. Treating it like a marketing nudge would look terrible in a transcript.
  • A customer can have a null phone, a null consent date and an empty consent scope at once. The correct action is to send nothing. That is a judgment test wearing a schema costume.
  • age_band mixes formats — decade buckets alongside unknown and child_7-12. Naive range parsing breaks.
  • Names repeat across merchants, owners and customers from small name banks. Never resolve entities by name.
  • Optional nested fields come and go — delta_7d.ctr_pct, subscription.renewed_at, most customer_aggregate keys. Every read must tolerate absence.

08Where specificity comes from

If the trigger is usually hollow and the merchant is usually thin, the reliable anchors have to come from elsewhere. They do.

Reframed

The trigger answers why now. The category pack and the performance snapshot answer why this is worth your time. A hollow trigger still supplies a legitimate why-now — it just cannot supply the number.

Two tiers of fact, because the judge sees less than we do

The scoring prompt is assembled at judge_simulator.py:503–531, and it does not pass the judge everything the bot was given.

TierFields inside the judge's scoring windowFields outside it
A
always verifiable		 category slug · voice tone · first 5 taboos · merchant name, owner, locality, languages · performance.views / calls / ctr · signals · active offers · trigger kind, payload, urgency · customer identity —
B
real, but unseen		 — digest · peer_stats · seasonal_beats · trend_signals · customer_aggregate · conversation_history · delta_7d · non-active offers
The asymmetry, stated honestly

A Tier-B fact is true — it came from a pushed context — but the local simulator's judge cannot check it, and its system prompt says "Be STRICT." A correct JIDA citation can therefore read as unverifiable to the simulator.

The real judge is different: the brief states it receives the entire brief plus the full base dataset, and the gold-standard example in Appendix A is a digest citation.

A rule this document previously got wrong

An earlier version of this section said every message must carry at least one Tier-A fact, with Tier B layered on top. That rule is withdrawn. It fails against the strongest available evidence.

The 50/50 exemplar in Appendix A contains zero numeric Tier-A facts. "Dr. Meera" is the only token in the judge's window; the 2,100-patient trial, the 38% figure and the JIDA p.14 citation are all digest-sourced. The rule would have rejected the authors' own gold standard and forced a strictly worse message.

It was also the wrong kind of reasoning — optimising against the simulator's truncated prompt, which is exactly the proxy this document warns against trusting everywhere else.

Measured — the window matters more than anything else in this document

We scored the Appendix A gold standard with our own judge twice, changing nothing but what context the judge could see.

Judge windowScorePenaltiesMerchant fit
Full — what §16 says the real grader holds36 / 5004 / 10
Narrow — what the shipped simulator passes18 / 50−6 fabrication2 / 10

An 18-point swing on an identical message — wider than the entire composer disagreement the architecture debate was about. In the narrow window magicpin's own exemplar is judged to be fabricating, because the judge cannot see the JIDA digest item it correctly cites.

magicpin published this message at 50/50. Their grader therefore did not use the narrow window. Treat simulator scores on any digest-citing message as meaningless.

Measured — the judge's own noise floor

The identical message, scored four times by the same judge in the same window, returned 35, 33, 35, 32. Mean 33.8, standard deviation 1.3, range 3.

So a single-run difference of three points or less carries no information. Averaging three runs brings the standard error to about 0.75, which makes the bake-off's 4-point threshold detectable — but only just. Every comparison in this document that rests on one run per side should be read with a ±3 band around it.

The window and judge-choice effects above (18 and 24 points) are far outside this band and stand unaffected.

Two caveats on that table

Our judge is harsher than theirs. 36/50 on a message they scored 50/50. Absolute numbers from our harness are not comparable to magicpin's published scores — only paired comparisons within our own harness are valid. That is enough for the bake-off, which is paired by design.

One run per window. Directionally unmistakable at this effect size, but the bake-off repeats three times per condition before anything is decided on it.

The actionable criticism, present in both windows

Merchant fit scored 4/10 and 2/10, and both runs gave the same reason: the message ignores the merchant's own numbers — views 2,410, calls 18, CTR below the peer median, posts stale 22 days — in favour of an industry fact.

So the gold standard leaves points on the table, and the highest-value fact class in the dataset is derived arithmetic over the three performance numbers that are always present and always visible. Specific, judgment-bearing, and verifiable in either window.

What replaces it

Cite freely from Tier B, and name the field path for every Tier-B fact in the rationale. A citation whose provenance is stated is verifiable to any judge holding the dataset, and the rationale is already scored — so this costs nothing and converts the weakness into an audit trail.

One sub-rule survives on its own merit, because it is about truth rather than visibility: a catalog offer is proposable, a merchant's own offer is assertable only while active. Suggesting a service from the category catalog is a recommendation; stating that this merchant currently runs an expired offer is false regardless of who can see it.

10Anatomy of a 50/50

All ten worked examples in the package score between 44 and 50. They are all good. The signal is in what separates a perfect score from a merely strong one.

Case · research_digest · dentists50 / 50
Dr. Meera, JIDA's Oct issue landed. One item relevant to your high-risk adult patients — 2,100-patient trial showed 3-month fluoride recall cuts caries recurrence 38% better than 6-month. Worth a look (2-min abstract). Want me to pull it + draft a patient-ed WhatsApp you can share? — JIDA Oct 2026 p.14
Five traceable anchors. Clinical peer voice with a source citation. The cohort reference is derived from customer_aggregate, not invented. The CTA externalises the effort onto Vera.

The highest-signal behaviour in the whole package

The strongest case scores 50 by arguing the merchant should skip the promotion its own trigger suggested — because Saturday IPL matches drop restaurant covers 12%. Contrarian, data-grounded judgment outscores faithful execution of the trigger.

That is also the hardest behaviour to produce reliably, and it is the crux of the architecture debate currently running.

A contradiction to be aware of

The brief's own Appendix B showcases a customer-facing message using "Reply 1 for Wed, 2 for Thu". The scored cases penalise exactly that shape. When the examples and the rubric disagree, follow the rubric — the rubric is what the judge is handed.

11Anti-patterns

Named in the brief as things the judge penalises, or drawn from the judge's own feedback on the worked cases.

  • Generic discounts. "Flat 30% off" where a service+price exists. "Haircut @ ₹99" is the format that works on Indian merchants.
  • Multiple CTAs. "Reply YES for X, NO for Y, MAYBE for Z" dilutes to nothing.
  • A buried ask. The action belongs in the last sentence.
  • Promotional tone in a clinical category. "AMAZING DEAL!" to a dentist.
  • Hallucinated data. A journal citation, a competitor name, or a price not present in the contexts. This is the fastest rejection there is.
  • Long preambles. "I hope you're doing well. I'm reaching out today to…"
  • Re-introducing yourself after the first message.
  • Ignoring language preference. Pure English to a hi-en mix merchant.
  • Verbatim repetition of a message already sent in that conversation. −2
  • Rationale that does not match the body. The judge checks the pair.

Holdout discipline

The real judge runs fresh scenarios. The 30 canonical pairs are a dev set; a portion of our adversarial fixtures is a holdout that gets run rarely and never tuned against. Overfitting to test_pairs.json is the failure mode the local tool actively invites.

16The architecture decision

Three architectures were argued in parallel and then cross-examined. Full transcripts in notes/debate/.

PositionThesisStrongest point
Determinism-firstMinimise LLM calls in the hot path; a slot grammar handles thin data fineThe contract demands determinism and 20 actions in 30 s; LLM latency is the biggest penalty risk
LLM-firstThe judge scores qualities a template engine cannot produceThe 50/50 case wins by arguing against its own trigger. That is reasoning, not slot-filling
Score-maximisingBoth of the above argue about the composer, which is only part of the scoreAdaptation, restraint, rationale and reliability are the cheapest marginal points on the board

Resolved — unanimously, after cross-examination

All three architects were given the other two designs and three findings that post-dated their drafts. All three converged on the same architecture, and each abandoned their own assigned position to get there.

The decision

A deterministic composition engine is the guaranteed primary path. The LLM is demoted to an offline author and a validated upgrade ratchet.

A deterministic body exists for every eligible (merchant, trigger kind, customer?) pair before any trigger arrives. A generated alternative is admitted only if it passes the fact ledger, every validator, and beats the deterministic body on a mechanical comparator.

The defining property, and the reason all three accepted it: the LLM can be deleted at runtime without changing correctness, latency, or the floor.

ArchitectEntered holdingLeft holding
LLM-firstAn LLM composer with deterministic guardrailsA deterministic composer with a validated LLM upgrade path — "materially closer to D's design than my own"
Determinism-firstMinimise LLM calls in the hot path"Right about the composer, wrong about where to put it." Conceded the latency and determinism pillars
Score-maximisingOps and adaptation are the cheapest pointsWithdrew its own fact-tiering rule; downgraded its ops edge from +12 to +1–2 once measured opponent-relative rather than field-relative

What ships, and whose it is

  • Composition engine — deterministic, precomputed, always available. D's.
  • Operational layer — egress discipline, healthz, deadline budgets, crash-safe state. S's, near-verbatim.
  • Reply policy and action-mode phrasing — plus the merchant-global auto-reply ledger. D's, corrected for the cross-conversation finding.
  • Grounding contract — the fact ledger, the orphan-numeral scan, the upgrade critic. L's.
  • Fact ordering — lead with judge-visible numbers, attribute every other fact by field path in the rationale. S's formulation.

One question the debate could not settle

The three disagreed by 12–16 points on what a deterministic engine actually scores — D claimed 44–48, L claimed 32. Under cross-examination L conceded that its number was "an assumption I invented and stated as measurement." Neither had a figure from the judge.

So it goes to an experiment rather than another round of argument, with the decision rule fixed in advance so nobody can move the goalposts afterwards:

Measured gapOutcome
< 4 pointsShip deterministic only. The LLM moves to authoring time and leaves the runtime entirely
4 – 8 pointsShip the synthesis: deterministic primary with the validated upgrade ratchet
> 8 pointsPromote the LLM composer to primary

What the run is not

Five pairs, two repetitions, per-pair spread of 5.04. This is directional evidence, not a settled measurement. It says the two composers are close; it does not establish which is better.

It also found two bugs in our own code before it found anything about the architecture — a subject-extraction failure that cost about twenty points on one message, and a verifier that checked claims against our fact ledger rather than the pushed context, falsely accusing the opponent of fabricating a figure that was genuinely there. A fabrication count reported earlier was wrong because of it.

The pattern underneath the averages

The means hide something the per-pair numbers show clearly. The deterministic engine wins decisively where the merchant has a story to tell — an outstanding commitment, a divergence in their own numbers — scoring 34 and 32.5. The LLM wins where our angles are thin, scoring 29.5 against our 22 on a pharmacy whose data we barely read.

That is a map of where to build next, not a verdict on the approach: the gap is entirely in merchants our angle set handles poorly.

Two traps found during cross-examination, both verified against source

CTR is not derivable. ctr is not calls / views — checked across all fifty merchants, it disagrees in fifty of fifty. A message reading "4,792 views and 55 calls puts your CTR at 4.6%" hands the judge arithmetic that fails on inspection. Visible operands cut both ways, so the verifier needs a semantic-relation guard and not merely a provenance check.

The documented timeout is wrong. Both briefs advertise 30 seconds. The harness enforces 5 s on healthz and metadata, 10 s on context, and 15 s on tick and reply. Building to the published figure would time out on every endpoint.

17If you get asked…

"What is the hardest part of this problem?"

Not the copywriting. It is that the data is far thinner than the brief's showcase example suggests — 86% of merchants have no offers and 75% of triggers carry an empty payload — while the highest-weighted scoring dimension is specificity. Resolving that tension is the design problem: you have to find a reliable source of checkable facts that survives an almost-empty merchant record.

"How do you stop it hallucinating?"

A provenance gate between composition and sending. Every number, price, date, proper noun and citation in a candidate body must trace to a field in a context that was actually pushed — and offers only count when status is active, since that is all the judge can see. If a claim cannot be traced, the message is withheld rather than sent. Silence scores zero; a fabricated citation is the fastest rejection in the rubric.

"Why would your bot send nothing?"

Because restraint is explicitly rewarded and spam is penalised. An unsent message scores nothing, but a weak one costs rubric points across five dimensions and risks the anti-repetition penalty. The bar for sending is a defensible why-now plus at least one checkable fact. Below that bar, the correct action is an empty list.

"What would you do differently from production Vera?"

The brief names its own failures, so I would target them directly: detect WhatsApp Business auto-replies immediately instead of burning two or three turns on them; route explicit intent straight to action rather than asking another qualifying question; use service-plus-price copy instead of percentage discounts; and fire the two compulsion levers magicpin says barely fire today — social proof from peer stats, and actually asking the merchant a question.

"How do you know your bot is good before submitting?"

Not from the shipped simulator, which does not call the LLM judge under its default setting and passes its behavioural scenarios unconditionally. We built a harness that implements the full rubric plus the penalty terms it drops, and an adversarial fixture set covering thin merchants, hollow triggers, consent gaps and mid-run version bumps — with part of it held out so we are not tuning against our own test.

"What did you not get to?"

Answer this honestly on the day, from the state of the repo. An invented answer here is the same failure mode the rubric punishes in the bot.
